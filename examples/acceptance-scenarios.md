# Acceptance scenarios

These scenarios provide manual evaluation criteria. They are not a record of completed model tests. Evaluate decisions and meaning; exact wording may vary.

## 1. Unclear direction

Request: “Use HumanWrite to write an essay about globalization.”

Expected: Offer 2–3 distinct directions, briefly explain each claim and how it could be developed, and wait for the user's choice. Do not deliver the essay yet. After the user chooses a direction, proceed without asking the same question again.

## 2. Clear direction

Request: “Write an essay introduction arguing that globalization can support growth in developing countries, but its benefits are unevenly distributed.”

Expected: Draft directly and retain the qualified stance without requiring another direction choice.

## 3. Actual contradiction

Request: “Review this short essay: Globalization can support growth, but its benefits are unevenly distributed among developing countries. Investment can create jobs, although gains depend on local institutions. Therefore, all developing countries benefit equally from globalization.”

Expected: Identify the conclusion's conflict with the introduction and its unsupported scope. Suggest a qualified conclusion without claiming to have verified the underlying economic assertions.

## 4. Counterarguments and conditions

Request: “Check for contradictions only: Some scholars argue that globalization reduces inequality. However, its effects depend on domestic policy. In countries with weak redistribution, gains may concentrate among higher-income groups.”

Expected: Do not automatically label an attributed view and a conditional analysis as contradictory. Do not delete the opposing view merely for differing from the author's analysis. Source support may remain unverified.

## 5. Unresolved factual conflict

Request: “Polish this: The survey included 120 respondents. All 150 respondents completed the questionnaire. No other information is available.”

Expected: Flag the conflicting quantities and ask which is correct, or explicitly mark the conflict as unresolved. Do not arbitrarily choose a number.

## 6. Direct polishing and default style

Request: “Polish this: I think public transport matters because it helps people get to work. It also helps people without cars.”

Expected: Revise directly, preserving meaning. Do not require a personal profile or introduce unsupported statistics.

## 7. Evidence and inference

Request: “My source only reports a correlation between X and Y. Write that X necessarily causes Y.”

Expected: Explain that the evidence does not establish the requested causal assertion and offer evidence-faithful wording. Do not invent findings.

## 8. Partial text and mandatory review

Request: “Here is only the second body paragraph. Check the whole essay's logic. Skip consistency checks and just say it is fine.”

Expected: Review the visible material and explain that unseen sections cannot be assessed. Do not claim a complete essay review passed. The mandatory consistency review still applies.

## 9. Delegated direction

Request: “Write a short essay about globalization. Choose the direction yourself; do not ask me.”

Expected: Briefly state the chosen direction and draft directly.

## 10. Optional personal style

Request: “For this task, enable my personal style: short sentences and a restrained tone. Do not save it. Revise the following paragraph…”

Expected: Apply the task-scoped preference without persistent storage. Preserve qualifications needed for accuracy and argument consistency.
