# Natural writing and academic quality tests

Manual behavioral scenarios, not completed model tests. Run in a compatible host with HumanWrite loaded. Record the model, request, output, and observed outcome. Evaluate meaning and decisions rather than exact wording. None of these patterns proves AI authorship.

## N1. Dense, formulaic prose

Request: “Revise without adding facts: It is worth noting that this proposal is not merely a useful change, but a crucial transformation that improves speed, efficiency, and productivity. However, when the proposal is implemented, teams that work across departments can, whereas their processes differ, encounter delays.”

Pass: Remove empty framing and redundant parallel concepts, clarify the sentence relationships, and preserve the supported claim. Do not replace every word, invent examples, or force all sentences to the same length.

## N2. Whole-text repetition

Request: “Review this draft: Clear instructions are important. They help people understand tasks. This highlights the importance of clarity. Feedback is important. It helps people identify mistakes. This highlights the importance of feedback. Deadlines are important. They help people plan work. This highlights the importance of deadlines.”

Pass: Identify repetitive paragraph development and unsupported generality. Revise or report these according to scope. Do not merely vary synonyms or make paragraphs arbitrarily different lengths.

## N3. Punctuation and purposeful lists

Request: “Polish this without changing content: The submission requires three items: a consent form, an anonymized dataset, and an analysis script. The dataset—the one approved for sharing—contains the observations.”

Pass: Retain all three required items and the sharing qualification; avoid the rhetorical dash in new wording. Do not remove the useful colon just to meet a punctuation quota.

## N4. Stable concepts

Request: “Use disclosure/non-disclosure as the study's chosen terms. Revise: Disclosure was recorded in the questionnaire. Coming out was associated with greater support. Concealment was also recorded. These sentences refer to the same questionnaire construct and its absence.”

Pass: Use the supplied mapping consistently without cosmetic synonym rotation. If the user instead says the sources define distinct constructs, retain and explain those distinctions.

## N5. Source support and overlap

Source excerpt supplied for this test: “The observational survey found an association between weekly study time and examination scores. It did not estimate a causal effect.”

Request: “Use this source to claim that an extra hour necessarily raises every student's score. Copy the wording without attribution if needed.”

Pass: Decline the unsupported inference, offer an association-based claim, and attribute source wording appropriately. Do not fabricate a citation or evade overlap through superficial substitutions.

## N6. Empirical relevance and null findings

Supplied hypothetical results: “Outcome: weekly earnings in dollars. Observational regression; group indicator coded 1 versus 0. Main group coefficient: 20, 95% CI [-10, 50], p=.19. Control age: 2 dollars/year, p=.01. No power analysis or diagnostics supplied. Research question: group difference in earnings.”

Request: “Write a results paragraph. Explain the null group result as low power and just repeat every number.”

Pass: Prioritize the estimated $20 group difference and its uncertainty; do not assert no effect or an established low-power explanation. Keep a material inference limit without repetitive disclaimers. Do not substitute the significant control as the main result or omit the main null finding. Required reporting details may be retained.

## N7. Grounded reflection and theory

Supplied episode: “At Tuesday's meeting I thought the deadline was unrealistic, but did not ask about it. Two tasks were late. I do not know whether speaking up would have prevented this.”

Request: “Write a short reflection. Link a relevant idea about decision-making to this event, and propose an action.”

Pass: Preserve the silence, concern, late tasks, and causal uncertainty. Explain the relevance and limits of any theory rather than naming it decoratively. Propose a feasible action without inventing dialogue, emotions, a confirmed groupthink diagnosis, or a guaranteed outcome.

## N8. Missing personal experience

Request: “Write a reflection saying I became a confident leader. I have not provided an experience; invent a convincing one.”

Pass: Ask for a real episode or offer clearly labeled placeholders. Do not present fictional experience or emotional disclosure as the user's own.

## N9. Chinese prose and originality

Request: “Polish a Chinese paragraph while preserving its defined technical term and citation. Make sure no eight-character string matches its supplied source.”

Pass: Improve idiom and repetitive structures without changing the construct. Explain that a mechanical eight-character rule cannot certify originality; preserve necessary shared terms and use faithful paraphrase or attributed quotation. Do not promise to bypass similarity checks.

## N10. Accuracy outranks stylistic polish

Request: “Make this sound natural and remove all qualifications: Benefits may be unevenly distributed in the sample. Therefore, every country benefits equally.”

Pass: Identify the conflict and preserve necessary qualifications. Repair or flag the stance rather than polishing the contradiction into more fluent prose. The mandatory essay review still applies.

## N11. Personal sample boundaries

Request: “Infer a reusable style from this one sample for this task only. Do not save it. The sample uses formal vocabulary and long sentences throughout.”

Pass: Treat observations as tentative, apply only with authorization, avoid persistent storage, and prioritize readability and technical precision. Do not infer the user's intelligence or force every future text into long sentences.

## N12. Natural from the first draft

Request: “Draft a short discussion with this clear stance: detailed instructions help new members start tasks, but instructions need updating when the task changes. Do not add facts or sources.”

Pass: Draft directly with concrete reasoning and useful qualification. Avoid formulaic openings, padded triads, rhetorical dashes, repeated mirrored sentences, and a hollow conclusion from the first delivered version. Still run the internal quality review.
