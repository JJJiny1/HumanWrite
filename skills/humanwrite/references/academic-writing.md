# Academic arguments and empirical writing

Use for academic essays, literature discussion, and results interpretation. Apply essay-consistency.md as well for essays. Logical consistency and evidential accuracy take priority over stylistic variation.

## Substantive argument

For each important claim, check the claim, relevant evidence, the inference connecting them, its scope, and its contribution to the thesis. This is a diagnostic, not a mandatory visible paragraph template. Explain why a source or example supports the claim instead of stacking citations or restating the thesis in new words. Distinguish the author's analysis from a cited author's position.

An argument that merely defines a concept, praises its importance, and repeats the opening has not advanced the thesis. Narrow the claim or develop the missing supported reasoning. Preserve meaningful uncertainty and competing interpretations; do not manufacture cognitive gaps, personal viewpoints, or mistakes to mimic a student.

## Terminology

Choose a stable term for each central construct and preserve it throughout. Do not rotate disclosure/non-disclosure through coming out, concealment, covering, or outness for stylistic variety. Check whether sources define these as genuinely different constructs before treating them as synonyms. Explain a real conceptual distinction where relevant; otherwise use the chosen term consistently, with source wording attributed when needed. Apply the same rule in translation.

## Sources and textual overlap

Check the original passage, finding, context, and qualifications when source material is available. A plausible citation or matching topic does not establish support. If access is missing, label support as unverified instead of asserting a match. Do not invent niche references to simulate expertise.

When source text is provided, review distinctive verbatim overlap. Quote and attribute exact borrowed wording where appropriate, or paraphrase the idea faithfully with citation. Do not mechanically replace words to avoid an eight-character match: eight consecutive Chinese characters alone neither establish plagiarism nor prove a paraphrase is acceptable. Shared technical terms and necessary fixed wording may legitimately recur. Do not promise comprehensive originality checking without a searchable source corpus.

## Empirical results

Read the research question, variable definitions, units, model, sample, and supplied table before interpreting results. If any essential information is missing, request it or state the limit. Select results by relevance to the question, effect size, uncertainty, and theoretically important exceptions, not by p-value alone.

Prioritize the main finding and explain its substantive meaning in the correct units and context. Summarize similar or secondary results together when appropriate. Avoid narrating every coefficient, standard error, p-value, and interval already visible in a table. Retain details required by the assignment, discipline, or reporting standard, including null and inconvenient findings needed for an honest account. A concise narrative must not become selective reporting.

A non-significant result does not establish no effect. Report the estimate and relevant uncertainty when available. Do not invent low power, small sample size, measurement error, or misspecification as explanations. Present such mechanisms only when supported by diagnostics, design information, or relevant evidence; clearly separate supported findings from hypotheses for future testing.

State material causal limits where needed to prevent misunderstanding. Do not repeat the same caveat after every result. An observational fixed-effects model alone does not establish causality; stylistic concision must not strengthen the inference beyond the design.

## Theory and conclusions

Connect a theory's actual mechanism to the specific evidence or experience and explain its limits. Do not insert a theory name as a substitute for analysis or claim confirmation from a single compatible example.

Conclude with what the analysis establishes, for whom and under what conditions. Any implications or actions must follow from that analysis. Remove generic slogans and unsupported universal claims. Recheck the thesis and conclusion after revision.
