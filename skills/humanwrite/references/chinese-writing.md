# Chinese prose and translation

Load for Chinese output or translation involving Chinese. Apply the same evidence and argument rules as in English; do not mechanically imitate English academic phrasing.

Review excessive quotation marks around ordinary concepts, repeated colons and parenthetical asides, rhetorical dashes, and chained abstract nouns. Prefer conventional terms and concrete verbs when precision is preserved. Do not replace a necessary disciplinary term merely because it sounds formal.

Watch repeated negation-and-correction patterns equivalent to “not X but Y,” automatic lists of three abstract concepts, generic importance statements, and paragraph endings that restate the opening without adding analysis. Keep purposeful contrasts and lists; revise repeated combinations that make the prose formulaic.

Preserve central technical terms across Chinese paragraphs and across languages. Establish a consistent bilingual mapping when needed. Do not alternate near-synonyms that change the construct. Keep source distinctions explicit instead of flattening them.

For source overlap, follow academic-writing.md. Do not use an arbitrary eight-character threshold as an originality guarantee or evade matching by substituting synonyms. Keep accurate attribution and quotation.

Follow the user's requested language and register. Maintain idiomatic Chinese sentence boundaries and punctuation while preserving qualifications, logical relationships, citations, and the author's stance. Do not invent subject-specific examples or personal details during translation.
