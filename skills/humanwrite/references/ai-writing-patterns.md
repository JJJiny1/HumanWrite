# Natural writing patterns

Use during planning and drafting, then review the whole text before delivery. These are editorial signals, not evidence of AI authorship. Diagnose repeated combinations in context, not isolated words or punctuation. Do not introduce errors, unsupported digressions, or artificial unevenness to appear human.

## Sentence load and vocabulary

Separate independent claims when nested clauses obscure their relationship. Keep a longer sentence when it expresses a necessary qualification or comparison clearly. Review repeated whereas, when, therefore, and however: make the relationship explicit once and remove connectors that do no work. Do not impose a fixed sentence-length quota.

Use direct, familiar academic language by default. Match an explicitly requested level or authorized sample without guessing ability from nationality or degree. Keep technical terms necessary for precision. Replace ornamental vocabulary when a simpler word has the same meaning; do not blacklist a useful word simply because models use it.

Review fillers such as “it is worth noting,” “this highlights the importance of,” “a multifaceted landscape,” “crucial,” and routine “in conclusion.” Retain a term only when it contributes a precise meaning. For Chinese equivalents, use chinese-writing.md when relevant.

## Punctuation and parallelism

By default, avoid rhetorical em/en dashes in newly drafted prose. Prefer a full stop, comma, or a concise rewrite. Preserve quoted punctuation, required notation, legitimate hyphens, and user-mandated style. For faithful translation or minimal editing, preserve source meaning and the requested degree of intervention.

Use semicolons sparingly when two closely related independent clauses benefit from them. Review repeated colons, parentheses, and quotation marks; remove decorative quotation marks and unnecessary asides while preserving direct quotations, citations, definitions, and punctuation needed for accuracy.

Review repeated “not X but Y,” “not merely,” “not only X but also Y,” “this does not mean,” and mirrored sentence halves. State the intended claim directly unless the contrast resolves a real misconception. Keep essential qualifications, but consolidate repeated caveats rather than attaching the same defensive disclaimer to every result.

Check lists of three causes, effects, adjectives, or concepts. Keep all three when each adds a distinct supported point. Remove padding and overlapping items; use the number the argument needs. Never replace all triads with another fixed count.

## Whole-text rhythm

Compare paragraph openings, lengths, endings, and reasoning roles across the document. Repeated general claim → example → general restatement, identical transitions, and equally expanded paragraphs can hide weak development. Organize each paragraph around its actual contribution. Expand difficult reasoning; keep limited points brief. Do not shorten or lengthen paragraphs merely to create visual variation.

For every paragraph, identify what it adds to the thesis. A polished sequence of claims is insufficient if later sentences only restate earlier ones. Remove circular restatement or add the missing supported reasoning. Preserve an assignment's required structure and reporting conventions.

## Specificity and revision

When a claim feels vague, identify what information would make it useful: a concrete mechanism, bounded example, result, source, or explanation of implications. Use supplied or verified material only. If it is missing, qualify or remove the claim, or ask for the minimum needed information. Never invent cases, numbers, references, or personal details to make prose feel authentic.

Example:

Before: “It is worth noting that globalization is not merely an economic phenomenon, but a multifaceted process that shapes political, social, and cultural dimensions.”

Possible revision: “Globalization also affects domestic politics. [Add a relevant, sourced example and explain its connection to the thesis.]”

The bracketed text is a drafting placeholder, not finished prose. Without supporting material, omit the unsupported expansion or explain what evidence is needed. The shorter sentence is useful only if the paragraph then develops a relevant claim.
