# Mandatory essay consistency review

Use for drafting, revising, or reviewing an essay. Apply proportional depth to the available text. If only an excerpt is provided, check it and explicitly limit any whole-essay conclusion.

## Argument map

Track a compact working representation, without exposing hidden reasoning:
- Thesis: the author's central claim, scope, conditions, and degree of certainty.
- Paragraphs: each main claim, its role in the thesis, and supporting evidence.
- Counterarguments: whose view is represented and how the author responds.
- Definitions and facts: important terms, dates, quantities, populations, and contexts.
- Conclusion: what the preceding reasoning actually supports.

## Five checks

1. **Thesis and conclusion:** Does the conclusion answer the original question with the same qualified position? It must not generalize beyond the body or introduce a new unsupported thesis.
2. **Claims across paragraphs:** Identify claims that cannot both hold for the same subject, time, conditions, and meaning. Check scope words such as all, some, always, may, necessary, and sufficient.
3. **Facts:** Compare repeated dates, numbers, names, and factual assertions. Verify conflicting facts against available reliable sources. Never pick a value merely to make the text consistent; request clarification or mark the conflict when unresolved.
4. **Concepts:** Keep definitions stable or explicitly signal and justify a change in meaning. Do not merge distinct concepts simply because their labels resemble one another.
5. **Evidence and inference:** Check that evidence supports the precise claim and scope. Do not turn correlation into causation, a local example into a universal claim, or an author's qualified finding into certainty. Inspect original sources when available; disclose when support remains unverified.

## Distinguish opposing statements

- Contradiction: two incompatible assertions under the same conditions, both endorsed by the author.
- Counterargument: an attributed competing view, evaluated or answered by the author. Preserve it and clarify attribution when needed.
- Nuance: outcomes differ by time, population, mechanism, or conditions. Make those conditions explicit instead of deleting complexity.
- Development: a view changes through the essay. It needs an explicit reason and a conclusion consistent with that development.

For example, “investment can create jobs” and “investment may increase dependency” can coexist. “Benefits are unevenly distributed” and “every country benefits equally” conflict when asserted for the same context.

## Resolve and recheck

Repair wording and links when the intended stance is clear. Preserve the author's position and evidence. If resolution requires choosing a new stance, changing an uncertain fact, or inventing support, flag the exact issue and ask the user, or provide conditional alternatives. After edits, recheck the thesis, affected paragraphs, and conclusion together. Never guarantee that a review has proved an essay free of every logical error.
